# 18Nov_NULL: Nobody lives here

The Netherlands is the most densely populated country in the EU, yet I suspected most of its land has nobody living on it, and I wanted to see how much. This map splits Dutch land into 100 x 100 m cells and colours purple every cell where nobody is registered as living. Cells where somebody lives stay white.

83% of the Dutch land surface has no residents at all: about 2.9 million of the 3.5 million land cells are empty.

Cities and villages are white holes in a purple sheet. Ribbon villages appear as thin white threads along dikes and roads in Groningen, Friesland and the Green Heart. The large purple blocks are the Veluwe, the newer Flevoland polders, the Biesbosch, the dunes and nature reserves, and the Wadden islands outside their villages.

Data: CBS Vierkantstatistieken 100 m (2025, v1) and the CBS/PDOK Wijk- en buurtkaart 2024 (land part of the municipalities).
