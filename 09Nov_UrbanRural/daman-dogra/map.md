# 9Nov_UrbanRural: Cows vs people

The Netherlands has 3.6 million cattle and 18.0 million people (2025), roughly one cow for every five people. I wanted to know where the cows actually outnumber us, because between municipalities the ratio varies enormously.

The map shows cows per person for each of the 342 municipalities. In the teal ones people win; Amsterdam, Rotterdam, Den Haag and Utrecht have less than one cow per hundred residents. In the purple ones the cows win, which happens in 24 municipalities (outlined):

- the Frisian dairy belt (Súdwest-Fryslân, De Fryske Marren, the Stellingwerven, Noardeast-Fryslân),
- the Overijssel and Achterhoek countryside (Staphorst 2.0, Tubbergen 1.9, Dinkelland, Hardenberg, Berkelland),
- the Gelderse Vallei, the veal-calf area around Barneveld (1.9) and Renswoude (2.6),
- Baarle-Nassau on the Belgian border, with 6.1 cows per person the highest of all.

The cow municipalities are spread over the north, east and south. What they have in common is dairy and veal farming on grassland and sandy soils.

Data: CBS StatLine table 80781ned (cattle, agricultural census 2025) and 03759ned (population on 1 January 2025); boundaries from CBS/PDOK gebiedsindelingen 2025.
