# 12Nov_Power: The new Dutch power grid

I wanted to see where the new wind power stands and where the grid that has to carry it runs. Every wind turbine in the Netherlands that OpenStreetMap knows about (3,525 of them) is a small warm point, and every high-voltage line of 110 kV or more glows teal underneath.

Offshore, 697 turbines stand in neat grids in the Dutch North Sea wind farms: Borssele in the south-west, Hollandse Kust and Egmond aan Zee off the Holland coast, and Gemini far to the north. On land they cluster where there is wind and space: the Flevoland polders, the IJsselmeer dykes, the Wieringermeer, and the open clay of Groningen and Friesland. The south and east have plenty of grid and very few turbines.

The heaviest lines connect the big cities and the old power stations, while much of the new generation sits on the coast and in the north. That mismatch is a large part of the grid congestion ("netcongestie") of recent years.

The dotted outline is the Dutch Exclusive Economic Zone, which decides which offshore turbines count as Dutch.
