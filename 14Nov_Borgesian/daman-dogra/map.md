# 14Nov_Borgesian: Borges's Chinese encyclopedia of Delft

In "The Analytical Language of John Wilkins" (1942), Borges quotes a fictional Chinese encyclopedia that sorts animals into 14 classes: those belonging to the Emperor, embalmed ones, tame ones, suckling pigs, sirens, fabulous ones, stray dogs, and so on.

This map uses those classes for all 39,711 OpenStreetMap buildings in Delft. Each class gets a playful, written-down rule based on OSM tags: the Nieuwe Kerk (burial place of the House of Orange) is the only building "belonging to the Emperor", listed monuments are "fabulous", apartment blocks are "innumerable", pubs are "frenzied", and anything under 15 m² "from a long way off looks like flies".

The result is as arbitrary as the original. The historic centre turns purple ("fabulous"), post-war estates turn blue ("innumerable"), and 13,443 garden sheds become flies. More than half the city (56%) falls through every rule into "et cetera".
